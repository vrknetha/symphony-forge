# Keep builds running in AWS while the laptop sleeps

6 parts · Risks: three powerful sign-ins (GitHub, Claude, Codex) sit on a box while a round runs; Anthropic's terms for the Claude token in the cloud are not confirmed; a box that dies before it writes the Codex sign-in back can leave an old one · New moving parts: sign-in secrets in your AWS account, a Codex sign-in lease in the AWS table, a bigger box size, a round script on the box, round results in S3

## What changes for you

In this plan, a "box" is an AWS machine from the earlier stories (4 processors and 16 GiB of memory, deleted after at most 3 hours). A "round" is one run of a builder (Claude, Codex or Neon) on one task or fix. "Story 1" is FORGE-PROVIDER-1 (the AWS setup, the machine limit and the lifetime rule). "Story 2" is FORGE-PROVIDER-2 (laptop test runs in boxes; a box works like a second laptop that Forge runs inside, with the laptop's lane rules). This story needs both.

- Build rounds can run in a box, so a round no longer stops when the laptop sleeps or shuts its lid. The laptop stays the one coordinator: it builds each round's brief, follows the round, runs every review and cold read, and decides the next step. Reviews and cold reads never go to a box. (Source: your decisions of 27 Nov 2026)
- It is off by default. A new client repo gets it off at setup. An existing client repo has it off after it upgrades and syncs, and its settings do not change. Both get the new steps in Forge's guide. To turn it on, the agent asks you, then a settings fix sets the new setting `box_rounds` in `forge.toml` to the families that may use a box, for example `["claude", "codex"]`. Story 2's setup must be done first. (Source: your decision of 27 Nov 2026 that it is off by default; the setting name is the planner's choice; confirm)
- A round starts on the laptop first, while the laptop's load is under half of today's limit and it has no swap in use; otherwise it goes to a box at once. This is the lower bar from story 2, and this story does not change it. (Source: your decision of 27 Nov 2026)
- Claude rounds use every box from day one. Each developer makes their own one-year token with `claude setup-token` and saves it in their own AWS Secrets Manager. The box reads it when the round starts and wipes it when the round ends. The box runs Claude Code unchanged. Forge never sees the token: it prints the exact command and the developer runs it, and Forge only checks that a value is saved. (Source: your decision of 27 Nov 2026; you accepted the terms risk, see Risks)
- Codex gets one sign-in at a time. Codex's own page for automation (https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/auth/ci-cd-auth) says to use one `auth.json` per runner or per serialized stream, and never to share the file across concurrent jobs or machines. So one Codex sign-in is "leased" (lent to one box until that box is done with it), and the refreshed file is written back to the secret store after each run, before the lease ends. A second Codex round runs on the laptop, or waits as it does today. The developer makes this sign-in once, in a terminal on a box (it is a new sign-in, not a copy of the laptop's file). (Source: your decision of 27 Nov 2026)
- One bigger box runs 3 to 4 Codex rounds at once through one Codex app server (the one Codex program that runs the rounds) and one sign-in. It has 8 processors and 32 GiB of memory, and it counts as one machine in story 1's limit. This is tried first. If OpenAI's rules or the real test say no, then in busy times a Codex round that finds the sign-in leased runs in a box with an OpenAI API key that the developer saved. API-key use is billed per use by OpenAI, outside the ChatGPT plan. (Source: your decision of 27 Nov 2026; "counts as one machine" and "only when the developer saved a key" are the planner's choices; confirm)
- Neon is tried: the developer signs in once in a box terminal, and Forge saves that sign-in like Codex's. If that works, Neon rounds can use a box. If it fails, Neon stays on the laptop, and a Neon round that cannot use a box runs on the laptop with one line that says why. (Source: your decision of 27 Nov 2026)
- The box pushes the branch with the developer's own GitHub sign-in, read from their secret store. That is wider access than the Actions machines of story 1, which hold no GitHub sign-in. (Source: your decision of 27 Nov 2026; you chose the wider access knowingly)
- When the laptop is away, the round finishes in the box, pushes the item's branch, and saves its log and result in S3. When the laptop wakes, `forge work <item>` reads the result, does not start a second round, and the item goes on to its review as after any round. The box keeps its own heartbeat while the round runs. The 3-hour hard maximum stays; at 2 hours 45 minutes the box stops the turn, pushes what is committed, and says time ran out, so the next round can go on. (Source: your decision of 27 Nov 2026; the 2 hours 45 minutes cut is the planner's choice; confirm)
- Watching, stopping, questions and notes work as for a laptop round. The round's log shows on the laptop while it runs. `forge stop` ends the round; committed work is pushed first. A worker's question ends the round as now, and your answer (`forge work <item> --note "<answer>"`) starts a new round in a new box. Every box round starts a new conversation with the whole brief, so it uses more tokens than a continued one. (Source: your decision of 27 Nov 2026; the stop and log channel is the planner's choice; confirm)
- Cost, estimates only. A 4-processor box costs about $0.20 an hour; the 8-processor box about twice that; setup looks up the real price. A 40-minute round costs about $0.14 on a small box. Each saved sign-in costs about $0.40 a month in Secrets Manager (up to 5, so about $2). Logs and results in S3 are deleted after 14 days and cost cents. The monthly amount in story 1 is still only an email, and its worst-case line now uses the bigger box's price when Codex boxes are on.
- Not known until the first real run: how many rounds fit one bigger box, whether OpenAI accepts one app server with 3 to 4 rounds on one sign-in, whether the Codex refresh survives a box that dies, whether the Neon sign-in made in a box terminal works at all, how long a box takes to be ready for a round, and whether Anthropic accepts the token for this use.
- The success measure is the one from story 1 (median time from the start of an item to its ready pull request, a week before and after, with the AWS cost per item). `forge lanes --aws status` counts box rounds in the same minutes. (Source: your decision of 27 Nov 2026)

Design: none (no product screen or page. Sign-in steps print in the agent's terminal; the machine view and the pane show a box round as a row marked with the box.)

## Why

Every build round runs on the laptop, so a round stops when the laptop sleeps, and a busy laptop makes rounds and tests wait: from 30 Sep to 27 Nov 2026 the evidence includes load of 370 and 211, swap use, and a round waiting about 45% of its time for a place. Story 1 moves CI and story 2 moves test runs. This story moves the rounds themselves, so the work goes on when the laptop is away. How much sooner work is ready is not measured yet.

## Done when

1. **With the new setting unset, which is the default in a new repo and in an existing repo after upgrade and sync, no round goes to a box and Forge makes no new AWS call; with it set, a family whose sign-in is not saved, or a repo whose box setup from story 2 is missing, runs its round on the laptop with one line that says why; the sign-in command saves and checks each sign-in without Forge ever reading or printing a value; and turning AWS off deletes the saved sign-ins with the rest.**
2. **A Claude round runs in a box with the developer's own token, read there and wiped at the end and never in a log, an argument, S3 or the result; its log shows on the laptop while it runs; its commits reach the item's branch on the laptop; `forge stop` ends it with its committed work kept; and a worker's question and the owner's note work as for a laptop round.**
3. **One Codex sign-in serves one box at a time: a second Codex round while it is leased runs on the laptop or waits; the refreshed sign-in file is written back before the lease ends; and the lease ends only when the box says it is done or AWS confirms the box is gone, even if the box dies.**
4. **One bigger box runs three Codex rounds at once through one app server and one sign-in; and when a busy-time round finds the sign-in leased and the developer saved an OpenAI API key, it runs in a box with the key and never touches the sign-in file.**
5. **With the laptop gone after the round starts, the box finishes the round, pushes the item's branch with the developer's own GitHub sign-in, and saves the log and result in S3; when the laptop wakes, `forge work` takes that result without starting a second round, and the item goes on to review as after a laptop round; a round that reaches 2 hours 45 minutes is stopped by the box, pushed and reported as out of time.**
6. **A Neon round goes to a box only after the one-time sign-in made in a box terminal is saved, under the same one-box-at-a-time lease as Codex; otherwise it runs on the laptop with one line that says why.**

## Risks

These steps are sensitive or cannot be undone:

- While a round runs, the box holds the developer's GitHub sign-in, their Claude token and their Codex sign-in. The worker and the repo's test code run on the box with internet access and can read them. They are read once and wiped at the end, not hidden during the round. A bad instruction in the repo or in a web page the worker reads could send them out. The GitHub sign-in reaches every repo the developer can reach. You chose this knowingly; keep the box's AWS role and the secrets in an account that holds nothing else.
- The Claude token is a subscription sign-in, used from a cloud machine in automation. Anthropic's terms for this use are not confirmed. You accepted the risk and will ask Anthropic. Anthropic can block the token at any time; a round then fails with the one line Claude Code prints, and the developer makes a new token.
- A Codex round that ends because AWS stopped the box may never write the refreshed sign-in back. The saved copy can then be old or no longer valid, and the next box round refuses until the developer signs in again.
- A round stopped by AWS before it pushes loses its uncommitted work, as turning off a laptop would not. The box pushes committed work at 2 hours 45 minutes, on `forge stop` and when the round ends.
- Codex and Neon sign-in files are made in a box terminal, and Neon's way of saving a sign-in on Linux is not documented; it may not work at all.
- Turning AWS off deletes the saved sign-ins and the S3 results. It cannot be undone.

### Shared details

- **Setting.** `forge.toml` key `box_rounds`, a list, default `[]` (off); entries from `repo.FAMILIES` (`codex`, `claude`, `antigravity`). It only says which families may use a box; story 2's setting says that boxes exist. The agent changes it only in a settings fix. Forge never edits `forge.toml`.
- **Secrets.** One Secrets Manager secret per sign-in, named `forge-<RepoId>/<name>` with `<name>` one of `github`, `claude-token`, `codex-signin`, `openai-key`, `neon-signin`, each carrying the tag `forge-runners=<stack name>`. They are empty shells in the story 1 stack (the template grows; the next `--aws setup` updates the stack through story 1's `deploy` step, so existing AWS stacks pick them up) so `--aws off` finds and force-deletes them with the App key. Token and key secrets hold the plain value. `codex-signin` and `neon-signin` hold JSON `{"files": {"<path under the box user's home>": "<base64>"}}`, at most 64 KB. Forge reads no value: it asks `aws secretsmanager list-secret-version-ids` for a current version.
- **Roles.** The box role from story 1 gains, for rounds only: `secretsmanager:GetSecretValue` on this stack's five secrets; `secretsmanager:PutSecretValue` on `codex-signin` and `neon-signin` only; `s3` get and put on the round prefix `rounds/*` of story 2's bucket (the builder reuses its bucket and its 14-day expiry; if story 2 has none for results, this part adds the prefix and the rule). The machine role's DynamoDB access stays limited to the box's own item.
- **Round files in S3**, under `rounds/<round id>/`: `request.json` (laptop writes), `log/<n>` (numbered chunks, each at most about 5 seconds of the driver's own output lines; the box adds a chunk only when there are new lines), `stop` (the laptop writes it; the box reads it every 5 seconds), `result.json`, `commits.bundle`. The round id is `<item with / as ->-<round number>-<6 random characters>`. The laptop's own record is a `box_round` field `{id, family, started}` in the item's existing record file (`codex._item_file(top, item, ".json", kind)`). `request.json`: `{family, item, branch, base, brief, model, effort, nudge, sandbox, keep_settings, auth}`; the brief is built on the laptop exactly as `worker._brief` builds it today. `result.json`: `{status: completed|failed|stopped|time_up, commit, pushed, final, nudged, uncommitted, ended}`; the box writes it last, after the push, the write-back and the wipe, so a result in S3 means the box is finished with the secrets. The laptop reads the same driver lines live and later, with the same code that `worker._run` uses today (`repo.claude_output`); only the line source changes.
- **Box script `round.sh`** (a part of the box's lane runner from story 2, run as the unprivileged round user), in this order: read `request.json`; put each secret into a folder in memory (`/dev/shm/forge-round-<id>`, mode 0700) or the driver's environment, never into the repo, the log or an argument; for a Claude round set `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` in the driver's environment only; git pushes through a credential helper that reads the `github` file from that folder; make the worktree from the code bundle (story 2's path) at `base`; run the driver (`claude_turn.py` with the pinned SDK Python; `codex_turn.py`; Neon's turn) with the request on stdin; upload log chunks and look for `stop` every 5 seconds; if the worktree is dirty after a completed turn, run the nudge turn with `request.nudge`; unless `keep_settings`, restore `forge.toml` to `base`; `git push origin <branch>` (never forced; a refusal sets `pushed` false); `git bundle create` of the new commits; for Codex and Neon, write the changed sign-in files back with `put-secret-value` before anything else is wiped; write `result.json`; wipe the folder and the environment. On `stop`, or at 2 hours 45 minutes after the box started (by the box's clock), it ends the driver's process group, then does the same steps with `status` `stopped` or `time_up`. A box item field `rounds_done` (the box's own item; the machine role may write it) is set after the last write-back.
- **Lease.** The item `lease#codex` (and `lease#neon`) in story 1's table, claimed by the laptop's placement call with `attribute_not_exists(pk)` and a `box` field naming the instance; freed by `tick.mjs`, only when the box's own item shows `rounds_done` after its last Codex round, or when AWS confirms the box is gone (the same confirmation rule as a place). A box cannot write a lease item (its role is limited to its own item), so nothing a box does can free one early.
- **Where a round goes.** The laptop decides, at the place where `machine.agent_slot` decides today (story 2 added the box branch there; this story reuses it and adds only the family and lease checks in `aws_rounds.choose`). It returns `laptop` with a one-line reason, or `box` with a box. A box round shows as a row marked `on <box>`, takes none of the laptop's places and counts against story 1's machine limit through story 2's admission. Reviews, cold reads and `forge close` never call it.
- **Laptop side.** `aws_rounds.py`: `choose`, `start` (writes `request.json`, hands the round to story 2's box runtime), `follow` (reads the next log chunk about every 5 seconds and yields its lines), `take` (reads `result.json`; fetches the branch from origin and checks that its tip is `commit`; if `pushed` is false or the tip differs, imports `commits.bundle`), `stop`. Hooks: in `worker._run` (Claude) and `worker._agy_turn` (Neon) a box round replaces the local process by `follow`; Codex uses the same replacement where `codex.run` starts its driver (the one change in `codex.py`, net zero lines). A pending `box_round` in the item's record makes `forge work <item>` go straight to `follow` and `take` and then through the same end-of-round code as a laptop round (the question check, the uncommitted-files warning, the timing record), and start no new round; the builder moves that end-of-round code out of the inline `finally` of `worker._work` into one function that both paths call.
